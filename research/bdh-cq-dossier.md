MISCONCEPTION TRAPS:
- FATAL: Claiming BDH-CQ's memory update is additive accumulation per demonstration. The paper's Eq. (1) is the GENERAL form S_t = U_θ(S_{t-1}, D_t); additive accumulation S_t = S_{t-1} + U_θ(D_t) is explicitly named as 'the special case' realized by linear attention. The DataForge brief's hint is a half-truth. Correct framing: 'the additive Hebbian form is the simplest realization and is what we can actually build and show you; the paper reserves the general case.'
- FATAL: Stating what 'CQ' stands for. It is defined NOWHERE — not the paper, not either Pathway page, not HF, not alphaXiv. Any expansion is fabricated.
- FATAL: Saying HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW effort = a specific number of latent iterations R. The paper never states R at any level and explicitly says update rules and implementation details are proprietary. Also, no absolute $ cost is given per effort level — only relative reductions (0%/11%/22%).
- FATAL: Conflating the two independent effort axes. Section 6.6 uses MIN/STANDARD on the deployed system (MIN = 1/3 the cost, -1.75pp, p=0.167). Section 7 uses LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH on a model separately trained across reasoning efforts (-22% cost, -8.5pp). They disagree in both magnitude and slope. Treating them as one curve is wrong.
- FATAL: Presenting $0.00070/task as a measured or billed price. It is 0.85 H200-GPU-seconds x an ASSUMED $3/H200-hour. Change the assumed GPU price and the headline changes proportionally.
- FATAL (and the best hook): Not noticing that the paper reports TWO different costs for the same 118/400 = 29.5% result — $0.00070 (abstract/Sec 5) and $0.00265246 (Sec 6.6 STANDARD). The paper never reconciles them. At the higher figure, '57x cheaper' becomes ~15x and '11x cheaper' becomes ~3x.

DIRECT ANSWERS TO THE TASK'S EXPLICIT QUESTIONS:

1) HOW IS CQ DIFFERENT FROM BDH? BDH is the architectural family (post-Transformer, ReLU-low-rank + linear attention, high-dimensional positive activations, recurrent associative state). BDH-CQ is a SYSTEM built on BDH layers adding (a) an interface for writing demonstrations into a recurrent contextual memory S at inference time with frozen θ, (b) a separate iterative latent workspace H refined R times conditioned on the final memory S_K, and (c) a surrounding pipeline of input transformations, candidate construction, and ranking. The S/H separation is the stated contribution. Sec 3.1-3.3, pp.3-4.

2) WHAT DOES CQ STAND FOR? Never defined. Anywhere. Do not guess.

3) IS THE MEMORY UPDATE ADDITIVE PER DEMONSTRATION? The brief's hint is HALF RIGHT and this is the most important correction I can offer. Eq. (1) is the general S_t = U_θ(S_{t-1}, D_t). The paper then says linear attention is 'the conceptually simplest standalone realization of linear correction rules on S, capturing the SPECIAL CASE S_t = S_{t-1} + U_θ(D_t)'. So additive accumulation is named as a special case, not as BDH-CQ's actual rule, which is proprietary. The team should build the additive Hebbian form (it IS what bdh.py's softmax-free, Q=K-nonnegative, tril() attention computes) and label it honestly as the special case the paper describes rather than as BDH-CQ's mechanism.

4) LATENT LOOP / HOW MANY ITERATIONS / WHAT IS REFINED? Eqs. (2)-(4): H_0 = E_θ(x*, S_K); H_{r+1} = F_θ(H_r, S_K) for r = 0..R-1; ŷ = G_θ(H_R). The workspace H is refined; the memory S_K is frozen during the loop. R IS NEVER GIVEN A NUMBER.

5) EFFORT LEVELS. Two independent axes, which must not be conflated. (a) Sec 6.6, deployed system: MIN vs STANDARD, $0.00088399 vs $0.00265246/task, 111/400 vs 118/400, difference -1.75pp, McNemar p=0.167 (statistically unresolved). (b) Sec 7 / Table 5, a separately trained model: HIGH 29.5% at 0% cost reduction, MEDIUM 27% at -11%, LOW 21% at -22%. No absolute dollars in Table 5. What physically changes at either axis is NOT disclosed.

6) PARAMETER COUNT: yes, 150M, stated repeatedly (abstract, Sec 1, Sec 5, Sec 9).

7) DOES EVALUATION-TASK DATA APPEAR IN TRAINING? For ARC-AGI-1 public eval: the paper explicitly says NO — 'Neither task identifiers nor evaluation-task demonstration pairs participate in training, and no parameters are updated at inference time' (Sec 1, p.2). For ConceptARC: YES — it is listed in the training mixture (Sec 4.2) and then used as an evaluation set (Sec 6.1), and Sec 6.5 concedes the control 'does not make ConceptARC a fresh benchmark, rule out exposure through training or checkpoint selection'. That asymmetry is a genuine, citable finding.

8) EXACT ARC-AGI-1 NUMBERS: 29.5% pass@2 = 118/400 tasks; pass@1 97/400 = 24.25%; test pairs 130/419 = 31.03% pass@2. Cost $0.00070/task computed as 0.85 H200-GPU-seconds at an assumed $3/H200-hour. CONFIRMED but with the unreconciled $0.00265246 figure in Sec 6.6 for the same run.

9) ARC-AGI-2: NO NUMBERS. Future work only. Same for ARC-AGI-3.

10) COMPARISONS: GPT-5.6 Luna (Low) 34.2% at $0.040 (ARC Prize, July 2026) -> 57x; at 20% of that after OpenAI's 30 July 2026 80% cut -> ~11x. HRM $1.48/task, TRM $1.76/task per ARC Prize, and both are TRANSDUCTIVE (backward-pass adaptation per task) whereas BDH-CQ is not — that is the sharpest architectural comparison in the paper (Sec 8, p.12).

11) CONTROLLED ARC-LIKE INTERVENTIONS: five distinct experiment families, all post-freeze. (i) Four monotone ladders — propagation distance 2-8 (48/48 perfect), copy multiplicity 1-4 (48/48 perfect), ordering length 3-8 (collapses to 1/24 at eight), nesting depth 1-5 (falls to 29/36 at five). (ii) Demonstration-coverage intervention with byte-identical test inputs (Table 3) — the cleanest causal result in the paper. (iii) Contextual binding of fresh color permutations, 2-8 simultaneous bindings, 96/96 correct. (iv) Composition with relocation (Table 4) — rotation 72/72, reflection 47/72, color swap 0/72. (v) Appendix ladders (Table 8) — conditional rule selection -43.3pp, parameter absent from demonstrations -30.0pp to 0%, panel union 2->3 panels -62.5pp, support chain -52.5pp. HARDEST CONCEPTS: ordering/sequence construction, nested containment beyond demonstrated depth, conditional rule selection via a marker cue, using parameter values not demonstrated (interpolation fails as completely as extrapolation), composing color swap, panel-union with three panels, gravity/stacking (but that last is confounded by task authoring).

12) LIMITATIONS the paper states about itself: proprietary internals and training recipe; small-n concept families with overlapping Wilson intervals; ConceptARC exposure not ruled out; generated-task authoring confound (GPT-5.6-authored, ρ=0.300 vs public, 24% duplicates, at least one internally contradictory task, 82.9% label agreement); each ladder condition is one puzzle family so results are within-family only; latent reasoning leaves no trace so 'a correct rule applied incompletely is observationally indistinguishable from a narrower rule applied completely'; and the MIN/STANDARD effort comparison is statistically unresolved.

RECOMMENDED FALSIFIABLE SENTENCE (my strongest suggestion, built from these findings): 'In BDH-CQ, in-context learning happens by WRITING demonstrations into a recurrent associative state with frozen weights — so adding one demonstration at the test complexity, with the test input byte-identical, changes the answer.' It is precise, it is falsifiable (the input does not change, so any behavioral change must come from memory), the paper gives exact ground truth (0/24 -> 13/24 ordering; 19/24 -> 24/24 nesting, Table 3 p.7), and the mechanism is reproducible in a toy in under a second via the parallel==recurrent Hebbian equivalence in the official MIT bdh.py.

BUILD CONSTRAINT (decisive): BDH-CQ cannot be run. No weights, no API, no demo. Every BDH-CQ number in the artifact must be a clearly-labeled replayed/precomputed value from arXiv:2608.09888. The live substrate must be either the official MIT pathwaycom/bdh forward pass at tiny scale, or an explicitly-labeled toy Hebbian memory. Both are legitimate under the brief's 'real tiny model / live toy system' standard; neither may be presented as BDH-CQ. pathwaycom/arc-task-gen (MIT, official) is a clean primary-source asset for generating ground-truth ARC-like tasks with a deterministic oracle.
