Demonstrations are weights
you write at inference time.

A frozen model adapts by adding rank-one updates to a fixed-size associative state, so changing only the demonstrations changes the answer — but that state's capacity is set by how much its keys overlap, not by its size, so the non-negativity that makes BDH inspectable is also what makes it forget sooner.

That is one sentence and both halves are falsifiable. The first half takes about sixty seconds — THE PRICE opens on a live 131K-parameter model that answers correctly, then answers confidently wrong when you delete one demonstration and leave the query bytes byte-identical. The second half is a slider, and a counterfactual that does not degrade.

Who this is for

A data scientist or ML engineer who can read a Transformer diagram and has not yet met a post-Transformer architecture. No BDH background is assumed, and nothing here needs a GPU, an install, or a sign-in — the models run in your browser tab.

Before you start

  • Attention as a matrix product — that QKᵀV is three multiplications. You do not need to have implemented one.
  • What a KV cache is, and why it grows with every token you generate.
  • That softmax normalises across a whole row. That single fact turns out to be the hinge of the entire argument.

You will leave able to

  • Say which of BDH's choices actually buys a fixed-size state — and which two only buy readability. Most people guess wrong.
  • Predict, from the weights alone, which neurons in a trained model will never fire on real text.
  • State what non-negativity costs, and reproduce the constant that sets the ceiling: 1/π.

What is live, what is not

live, in this tab

A 131,072-parameter BDH we trained on an in-context substitution cipher, running as plain typed arrays — forward pass, σ, the equivalence residual and the capacity sweep are all computed while you watch. web/public/model.bin, 257 KB.

ours, replayed

An 8M-parameter BDH that translates English into French, trained by one of us before this hackathon and reused with disclosure. Its activations, attention and synapse graph were exported offline by research/export_*.py and are replayed here.

replayed, cited

Every BDH-CQ figure, quoted from arXiv:2608.09888 with a section or table locator. BDH-CQ has no public weights and no public API, so nothing here runs it and nothing here is presented as its output. Nothing on any page is animated fiction.

Where this sits, and what each source is doing here

  1. Kosowski, Uznański, Chorowski, Stamirowska, Bartoszkiewicz (2025). The Dragon Hatchling. arXiv:2509.26507 — the architecture itself, and the source of the claim that non-negativity buys interpretable, sparse, monosemantic structure. That half is theirs; the price we put on it is ours.
  2. Engdahl et al. (2026). BDH-CQ technical report. arXiv:2608.09888 — in-context learning with recurrent latent reasoning. Cited for its equation (1) and the additive special case named in §3.2, which is exactly what the public BDH code computes. Replayed evidence only.
  3. Yang, Wang, Zhang, Shen, Kim (2024). Parallelizing Linear Transformers with the Delta Rule over Sequence Length. arXiv:2406.06484 — cited beside our second clause. Its abstract replaces “the additive update in linear transformers” with the delta rule and reports that variant “more effective at associative recall” — that additive update is precisely BDH's write, and the capacity ceiling we measure is why an alternative to it exists.
  4. Sun et al. (2024). Learning to (Learn at Test Time): RNNs with Expressive Hidden States. arXiv:2407.04620 — the same idea taken further: make the update rule a step of self-supervised learning rather than a fixed outer product. The contrast that shows BDH's write is the simple end of a real design space.
  5. Wang et al. (2025); Jolicoeur-Martineau (2025). Hierarchical Reasoning Model; Less is More: Recursive Reasoning with Tiny Networks. arXiv:2506.21734, arXiv:2510.04871 — the other route to test-time adaptation, and the reason this artifact takes the side it does: both adapt by optimising, a backward pass per unseen task, where BDH-CQ adapts in recurrent state with no parameter update. Worth reading for evidence discipline too — TRM reports 45% on ARC-AGI-1 and 8% on ARC-AGI-2, while BDH-CQ reports no ARC-AGI-2 result at all.
